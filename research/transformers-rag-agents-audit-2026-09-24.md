Audit date: **24 September 2026**. Repository snapshot: `06f5fe8`.

**Implementation follow-up, 27 September:** the gaps and corrections below have now been integrated into the lessons and quizzes. **Answer: partly.** These chapters teach many durable engineering conclusions, and RAG already includes several important research ideas. They do not yet provide adequate coverage of the 2024–25 research reviewed here or of the selected 2026 developments. Some missing items need new explanations; others need citations, qualifications, or corrections to existing explanations.

## What “most cited” means in this audit

I queried OpenAlex for 2024–25 publications, sorted by `cited_by_count`, using five title searches: `transformer`, `attention`, `retrieval augmented generation`, `agent`, and `language model`, with up to 50 records each. I supplemented those results with targeted title searches for relevant methods and benchmarks, and checked research claims against primary papers and proceedings pages. Surveys help establish coverage priorities; they are not treated as new experimental demonstrations.

**This does not establish an exhaustive global most-cited ranking.** Title searches miss papers, unrelated fields enter broad searches, preprint and proceedings records split citations, and some returned metadata was wrong. For example, an *Attention Is All You Need* record was dated 2025; it was excluded. A Semantic Scholar cross-check returned HTTP 429 twice. Unreliable or missing citation metadata is not evidence that a paper has little influence.

## Corrections to prioritize before expanding coverage

1. **Exact attention is not bit-identical attention.** [c3.2:14](../src/content/c3/c3.2.mdx#L14) says FlashAttention is bit-for-bit exact, while Part 6 correctly qualifies floating-point associativity. Make these consistent. The FP8 path in [FlashAttention-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08608) also needs its own numerical qualification.

2. **GQA is common, not universal.** [c3.2:131](../src/content/c3/c3.2.mdx#L131) generalizes GQA to every 70B-plus model after Llama 2. [DeepSeek-V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434) is already a counterexample, and the next paragraph mentions MLA. Replace the absolute statement and expand the actual distinction.

3. **Large batches do not erase MoE's sparse per-token arithmetic.** [c3.2:141](../src/content/c3/c3.2.mdx#L141) says the FLOP saving erodes because a batch touches most experts. The number of experts touched by the batch affects weight traffic and utilization; it does not by itself make each token run through all experts. Explain compute, resident memory, communication, and achieved speed separately, using [DeepSeek-V3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437) as a concrete systems example.

4. **Multiple agents have more than one possible benefit.** [c4.1:152](../src/content/c4/c4.1.mdx#L152) makes context isolation exclusive. The later parallelism advice is better. The [controlled scaling study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08296) and [diversity study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03794) support a conditional, measured account. They do not support “more agents is always better.”

5. **An open action space makes evaluation harder, not impossible.** [c4.2:157](../src/content/c4/c4.2.mdx#L157) confuses inability to enumerate all actions with inability to evaluate tasks. [SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793) and [OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972) provide execution-based counterexamples. Keep the sandbox and permission requirements; distinguish measured task performance from universal safety guarantees.

6. **Separate GraphRAG's measured limitations from hypotheses about bias.** [c5.2:267](../src/content/c5/c5.2.mdx#L267) correctly notes that comprehensiveness is not factuality. But the following paragraph asserts that output length explains part of the measured advantage without an identified length-controlled analysis. In the [paper's limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16130v2), fabrication-rate comparison is explicitly left as strengthening future work. Describe verbosity as a possible confound, and distinguish subjective-criterion validation from factual correctness. A graded criterion is not automatically unvalidatable merely because TPR/TNR are unsuitable metrics.

7. **Long-context economics need workload assumptions.** [c5.2:219](../src/content/c5/c5.2.mdx#L219) assumes caching cannot help a per-tenant corpus. Repeated queries within a tenant can still share a permitted prefix. Use measured hit rates, corpus updates, and cache isolation in the cost model. The [RAG/long-context comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833) also makes clear that cost and quality need separate comparisons.

## Suggested integration order

1. Correct the statements above and add primary-source links to conclusions already taught.
2. Extend **c3.2** with worked MLA cache reasoning and a compact dense/sparse/recurrent/hybrid comparison. Extend **c3.3** with test-time compute and a precision/hardware qualification. Distinguish R1-style post-training from inference-time search.
3. Extend **c4.2** with one execution-evaluated task, `pass^k` versus `pass@k`, and a reliability scorecard; update **c4.1** with measured multi-agent tradeoffs and user–agent coordination.
4. Extend **c5.2** with selective/corrective/learned retrieval, empirical RAG-versus-long-context routing, distinct graph designs, and a text/table or multi-hop evaluation example.
5. Add a dated, short emerging-research note for Engram, mHC, RLMs, and FP4 attention. Their inclusion should communicate mechanisms and evidence limits, not imply that all are standard production architecture.
6. Update the corresponding quizzes so they test the new distinctions and stop reinforcing the corrected absolutes. Keep detailed statistical machinery in c6 and cross-link it.

The course's practical structure is appropriate. The useful update is to attach research evidence and boundaries to engineering decisions, then fill the missing mechanisms—not to replace the lessons with a list of paper names.
